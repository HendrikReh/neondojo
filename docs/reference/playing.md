# Playing

How matches against GNUbg or NeonGammon, feedback modes and game results behave. For a guided introduction, see the [user guide](../user-guide/README.md#2-playing).

## Playing against an engine

For NeonGammon, the default strength is **2-ply** (including when switching to NeonGammon for a single match in **New match**). Choose **2-ply + Top-2 (10 mEq)** in **New match** for a single match or in **Settings → Playing** as the default for future matches; the active configuration profile saves this choice (`play_top_two`, migration `0088_play_top_two`). In contact positions where the two leading checker plays are at most 0.010 equity apart at 2-ply, NeonGammon re-evaluates those two plays at 3-ply and plays the better one. Standard 2-ply applies to checker decisions in other positions and to all cube decisions. **2-ply + Top-2 (10 mEq)** works with NeonGammon `3.0.0` and `3.1.0`, cannot be combined with a difficulty preset, and is preserved when a match is paused.

### NeonGammon difficulty presets

In **New match**, select **NeonGammon** as the bot engine and choose a preset
under **Playing strength**. Difficulty presets require the verified
**NeonGammon 3.1.0** wheel; on `3.0.0` they are disabled in setup and rejected
by the API with HTTP 422 (while 1-ply, 2-ply, and 2-ply + Top-2 remain
available). The presets are **Beginner**, **Casual**, **Intermediate**, and
**Full Strength**. Beginner, Casual, and Intermediate sample 1-ply checker
alternatives and close cube decisions. When the selected match length is 5
points, the preset group shows **5-point PR** estimates of about **20.3**,
**15.8**, **9.3**, and **1.0 PR**, respectively. All four presets were scored
on the same 6,912 decisions against the same rollout reference.
[Five-point preset PR benchmark](../open-sage-local.md#five-point-preset-pr-benchmark)

In new matches, **Full Strength** plays Stage 11 at **3-ply for checker moves**
and **2-ply for cube decisions**, following the engine's recommendation for
each. The match saves its preset (`engine.difficulty`) and checker depth.
On reload or resume, the match bar shows the saved preset, and Full Strength
matches started with the original 2-ply preset continue to play at their saved
depth.

API clients can omit `play_plies` when selecting a preset. For new Full
Strength matches, the former explicit value `2` is also accepted and the
session records the current checker depth (`engine.plies: 3`). Sessions also
report `engine.top_two` for the 2-ply + Top-2 strength.

### Start, resume, or end a match

Open **Playing** to start a 1–25-point match or choose **Money game**. **New match** starts with the active profile's last saved settings. During a match, **Resign game** and **Pause match** sit at the right of the match bar. The **More actions** menu (**⋯**) beside them holds **Forfeit match**, **New match**, and **Paused Matches**. Setup opens as a dialog over the playing view: the current match, or an empty board when none exists, stays visible but inactive behind it, while the app header remains usable. It offers your name, the bot engine (GNUbg or NeonGammon) and its playing strength, your color and the four play modes as radio choices, each mode with its description, and the match length as a list. Name, bot engine and strength start from **Settings** and apply only to the new match; the saved settings stay unchanged. Your choices of color, match length, play mode and automatic training analysis also apply to the new session. **White** plays the light checkers and **Black** the dark ones on every board, including BGBlitz themes. The gear beside the analysis and rules engine line opens **Settings → Playing**. **Start match** is the primary action; while a match exists, **Back to match** returns to it, or **Resume match** if it is paused. **Close** or Escape closes the dialog; without a match, **New match** above the empty board opens it again. If the last stored match no longer exists, Playing forgets it and opens setup instead of reporting an error. A pending profile conflict remains available for recovery under **Settings → Profiles** while you start a match. GNUbg handles opening rolls, legal moves, doubling, scoring, the Crawford rule, and match completion. NeonDojo automatically declines bot resignation offers, so play continues until a cube or bear-off result.

Money games use the Jacoby rule and ordinary doubling, with beavers disabled and no target score (`match_length: 0` in the API). Playing automatically generates a learning review after each completed money game. **Next game** carries the running score forward once the review is saved. **End session** in the same dialog closes the money session with its final score. The session keeps its games and reports and cannot be continued afterwards. Older single-game money sessions can also continue. Individual game reports appear under [Study → Reports](study.md#reports).

**Pause match** in the match bar pauses an unfinished point match or money session, including between games. NeonDojo saves the last confirmed checkpoint, including the score, dice, pending decision and engine settings. If your turn contains unsaved checker steps, the confirmation dialog lets you cancel and continue the turn or discard those steps and pause. Choose **Resume match** on the paused board or beside the entry in **Match database → Paused Matches**. **Paused Matches** in the setup dialog, above the empty board, or in the **More actions** menu during a match, opens that database for the current view. The same entry returns to its previous database. If that database was deleted or now contains a copy, NeonDojo tries **Bot Matches** and then **Unsorted**, preserving any existing copies.

NeonDojo automatically archives bot matches, including previously saved sessions, in **Match database → Bot Matches**. Manage individual matches there using the standard controls for names, tags, comments, moves between databases, copies, deletion, export, and analysis. Choose **Resume match** to return to a running original. Completed matches and independent copies open in Analysis. **Playing** restores the last session after reload and requires an explicit action to resume a paused match. **Download SGF** exports the match at any time.

The setup and match bar identify the **Bot engine**, **Analysis engine** and **Rules engine**. The bot engine evaluates the opponent's choices. The analysis engine evaluates decisions for hints, feedback and review. GNUbg runs the game rules with either bot, so it must include its embedded Python module. New matches record the engine selections, versions, depths and resolved GNUbg executable path. Existing matches keep the settings captured at creation. Starting a new Play session while a benchmark owns or is waiting for exclusive engine access returns a conflict immediately. Playing and analysis use separate NeonGammon workers. Their native evaluations share the [host CPU budget](settings.md#settings-and-engines) with GNUbg. For a NeonGammon turn, the native action has a three-minute deadline that includes waiting for engine access (for example behind an exclusive benchmark or the shared CPU thread budget). If an engine becomes unavailable, times out or fails, the saved checkpoint remains available for resumption.

### Rolls, checker moves, and the cube

Every game begins with an automatic opening roll on the board. Tied rolls animate in order, followed by the deciding pair. If you win the roll, you can start moving checkers with those dice. If the bot wins, its first move plays on the board. Reloading resumes from the saved checkpoint.

After the bot plays or passes because it has no legal move, its dice remain faded on the board and the game waits for your choice. Hover over the dice or the gap between them to lift both dice off the board visually. Click anywhere in that area to start your roll, or click the cube to offer a double when allowed. An available cube lifts and casts a shadow when you hover over it. The action panel beside the board always offers the same choices as buttons: **Roll dice** (Enter) and **Double** (D). When doubling is not allowed, **Double** stays visible but disabled, with the reason below it. If the Crawford rule, your opponent's ownership of the cube, or a cube value that already covers your remaining match points prevents doubling, the action panel explains why. When the bot doubles, the response dialog shows the current and offered cube values.

Your checkers are always at the bottom of the board. Your saved bear-off direction determines the side of your home board. Hover over or focus a checker to preview its destination, then click its point to move it. When only one die permits a complete legal turn from that checker, the click uses that die. If either die is legal there, the first die takes priority. Before moving, click the dice or the gap between them to swap their order. **Undo step** reverses one unconfirmed checker step at a time and shows the number of pending steps. The action panel stays accessible while scrolling and shows the current notation. When the turn is legal and complete and animation has finished, choose **Confirm turn** in the action panel, press Enter, or click the dice pair, including the gap. If you have no legal move, **Pass**, Enter or a click on the same area passes. You can also focus the pair and press Enter or Space. **Z** undoes a step, **H** requests a hint in the modes that offer one, and Escape returns from a reviewed move to the game. Each button shows its shortcut as a keycap on its corner and names it in its tooltip and shows the instructions for the current step. Hovering anywhere over an interactive pair lifts both dice and casts a shadow beneath them. When pressed, the interactive dice and cube settle briefly toward the board. Used dice fade. A partly used double fades exactly half of one die.

The backend supplies the complete checker paths for the saved roll, including the die used at each step and the index of the corresponding GNUbg move. The interactive board filters those paths as you choose steps. Resuming an older game derives the same paths from its saved position and legal moves.

The move history follows the latest saved action and uses the same row layout as Analysis, with notation, player colors, dice, and neutral analysis markers. It sits to the left of the board and moves below it on narrow screens. On large desktop windows, the board grows and the wider history scrolls alongside it with larger text and more visible moves. Selecting a historical move shows preview controls and **Return to game** beside the board. Because the board no longer shows the live position, it and those controls are framed in the accent color. The board stays in place. The game-end dialog shows the winner, points won, and the calculation when the record supports it. Choose **Next game** to continue. At the end of a match, **New match** opens setup and **Back to board** returns to the finished board.

### Decision dialogs and hints

Incoming cube offers, Training and Tutor feedback, and game or match results use a modal dialog when a decision is required. It opens over the center of the board. Drag its title to see the checkers underneath. The position is retained while the dialog is open and stays within the viewport when the window or dialog changes size. Keyboard focus stays inside, and the dialog requires a decision before you can use the board again. Escape and outside clicks leave it open. In Training and Tutor feedback, Enter chooses **Continue**, or **Play anyway** after a Tutor warning. Errors and pending acknowledgments appear inside the active dialog. Expand **Active mode** to access the mode and [Tutor warning threshold](#playing-feedback-modes).

**Hint** opens a movable dialog with its own loading and error states. Select a checker suggestion with the mouse or Up/Down to see the full saved turn on a separate, read-only board. Use **Initial**, **Arrows**, or **Final** to see the starting position, move arrows, or projected result. Cube hints are text only. **Close** or Escape returns to the game or to the required decision dialog. The live dice order and unconfirmed steps are preserved.

### Resigning

**Resign game** is available in the match bar before rolling and while moving checkers. It stays visible but disabled while the bot plays or a decision is pending. Choose a single, gammon, or backgammon resignation in the movable dialog. It uses the current cube to show the points awarded if accepted, the projected score, the target score for point matches, and whether the match would end. The bot engine decides whether to accept, and GNUbg supplies the resulting score, SGF, and move history. Cancellation or refusal preserves the checkpoint and unconfirmed steps. An accepted resignation opens the usual result dialog. Resolve any pending feedback before resigning.

**Forfeit match** is available in the **More actions** menu during an unfinished point match, in the resignation dialog, and in the dialog between games. Confirm to record a match loss and award the bot the points it needs to reach the target score. Confirmed moves and completed game results stay in the history. Unconfirmed moves are discarded only upon confirmation. The SGF export includes the conceding color in the NeonDojo property `BTFORFEIT` and preserves the played games.

### Animation and game sounds

The opening roll uses the same dice artwork and animation as regular rolls. When the bot opens, the actual opening dice stay visible while its checker moves play in sequence. Hits animate to the bar and bear-offs to the tray. The decision controls wait for playback, rapid legal source clicks queue in order, and each undo animates its reversal. Reload restores the saved checkpoint without replaying old moves. Leaving Playing stops playback, and reduced-motion mode uses short fades.

Apply **Settings → Playing → Game sounds** to update the current configuration and the browser’s playback preference immediately, including for an ongoing game. Both players use recordings from `sounds/`. `rollingDice.au` plays for rolls, `move.au` for moves and bear-offs, and `hit22.wav` for hits. A new bot double plays `double.au` once when the response dialog appears after checker playback. Loading a checkpoint, hovering, undoing, and confirming steps whose sounds already played are silent. Audio failure does not interrupt play.

The PCM WAV files bundled in `web/src/assets/play-sounds/` preserve the recordings' decoded samples. Run `python3 tools/prepare_play_sounds.py` with FFmpeg installed to regenerate them, then build normally. Playback uses the bundled files directly.

### Play API and persistence

| Method | Endpoint                                   | Purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| GET    | `/api/play/sessions`                       | List the 100 most recently updated sessions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| POST   | `/api/play/sessions`                       | Start a session with a request such as `{ "human_player": 0, "match_length": 5 }`. `0` is white and `1` is black; `match_length` is 1–25, or `0` for a money session. Optional `human_name`, `play_engine`, `play_plies` and `play_top_two` override the saved player name and bot profile for this session only. A blank name uses the saved one. `play_top_two: true` requires NeonGammon (`bgsage`) at 2-ply and cannot be combined with `play_difficulty`. An explicit engine or depth without this flag selects plain evaluation. An unverified or invalid bot profile returns 422. Optional `play_mode`, `tutor_threshold` and `training_auto_analysis` set the feedback mode and its preferences. Pass `play_difficulty` to select `beginner`, `casual`, `intermediate`, or `full_strength` with NeonGammon `3.1.0` (sessions report `engine.difficulty` and `engine.top_two`). |
| GET    | `/api/play/sessions/{id}`                  | Read a saved session by ID, including sessions outside the list limit.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| POST   | `/api/play/sessions/{id}/actions`          | Apply an action using the current `version`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| POST   | `/api/play/sessions/{id}/mode`             | Switch `play_mode` and optionally `tutor_threshold` for the current `version`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| POST   | `/api/play/sessions/{id}/feedback`         | Request a `hint`, `reveal` a solution or `retry` a decision in the learning modes.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| POST   | `/api/play/sessions/{id}/review`           | Generate the learning review after a completed match or money game.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| GET    | `/api/play/sessions/{id}/history?version=` | Read the board snapshots for the move history at that saved revision; any other revision returns HTTP 409.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| GET    | `/api/play/sessions/{id}/sgf`              | Download the saved SGF, including the escaped player name.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| POST   | `/api/play/checker-turns`                  | Return complete legal checker paths in absolute coordinates for a given `board`, `player` and `dice` (empty list for a forced pass), using the same step rules as live play and practice validation.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

Actions include `opening_roll`, `start_opening_move`, `roll`, `move`, `pass`, `double`, `take`, `drop`, `resign`, `forfeit_match`, `next_game`, `end_session`, `pause`, and `resume`. For `move`, send the zero-based index from the selected `legal_turns` path as the request’s `move` field. For `resign`, send `resign_level: 1 | 2 | 3` during a human Roll or Move decision. For `forfeit_match`, send only the action and current version. The completed point session returns `match_forfeited: true`. `pause` and `resume` change the session's `paused` status and database assignment atomically. `end_session` closes a money session after a completed game and sets `money_session_ended: true`. Stale or competing actions and attempts to play a paused match return HTTP 409. An unavailable decision or declined resignation returns HTTP 422 and preserves the checkpoint.

For native play, NeonDojo restores the saved SGF in an isolated GNUbg subprocess with a three-minute deadline, checks the exact saved GNUbg ID, and runs the bot until the next human decision. It holds no database transaction during engine work. An optimistic revision check saves the SGF and response snapshot together. GNUbg's complete 0-ply legal move list defines the allowed final boards at every bot search depth. The backend matches complete checker paths to the legal list and sends their individual die steps to the client.

## Game results in the move history

At the end of each completed game, the move history shows the winner, points won, the calculation from the result and cube, and the new match score. For example, **Gammon (2) × Cube 2 = 4 points** is a four-point win. This applies to imported matches in Analysis and to completed games in ongoing bot matches. Existing matches show results without reimporting or changing stored positions or evaluations.

A declined double uses the cube value before the offer. Resignations and results known only from the game record are labeled accordingly. If the next game's recorded starting score is the only evidence of points won, the calculation shows the difference, such as **6 − 2 = 4 points**. A win type appears only when the game record supports it.

## Playing feedback modes

New matches default to **Coach**. Choose any mode during setup. **Settings → Playing** stores the default mode and **Open training analysis automatically**. New matches capture the **Error** threshold from **Settings → Analysis** (0.020 by default) for Coach feedback and as the initial Tutor warning threshold. They capture these preferences and the analysis depth independently of the bot's playing strength. Older sessions saved before feedback modes use Competition behavior.

| Mode            | Feedback during play                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Competition** | Hides analysis, pip counts, error markers, and PR.                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Training**    | Shows full equities and win, gammon, and backgammon probabilities. With automatic analysis disabled, use **Hint** to request it.                                                                                        |
| **Coach**       | Shows equity differences and compact feedback using the move history’s severity colors.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Tutor**       | Warns before a checker or cube decision at or above the threshold. **Try again**, **Show solution**, and **Play anyway** preserve the first attempt. **Show solution** reveals the evaluation and any checker previews. |

A single match bar above the board groups the score, match format, game number, the engine roles, and the current turn status, followed by the play mode, **Resign game**, **Pause match** and the **More actions** menu. The bot engine tooltip shows its full version, playing depth, and bear-off direction. The analysis engine has its own depth label. The player rows above and below the board show each player's name, points, and pip count side by side. The match bar's **Play mode** selector switches an ongoing match immediately and saves the choice for reload. It preserves the current position and partial checker steps. In Tutor mode, edit **Tutor warning threshold** beside the selector, then leave the field or press Enter to save and recheck any pending warning. The saving indicator confirms success for three seconds. These controls also appear under **Active mode** in decision dialogs. Live changes apply to the current match. The default warning threshold for new matches comes from Settings → Analysis.

In Coach mode, a checker move at or above the captured **Error** threshold opens feedback before the turn is played. Choose **Confirm turn**, press Enter or click your dice again to play the move anyway, or open **Details** to inspect alternatives and try again. Moves below the threshold and forced passes play on the first click and are rated in the move history. The history rates the move that was played. When it replaced an earlier choice, the first attempt and its loss appear under the row, and the row names the engine and depth that analyzed the session. Clicking the opponent’s dice starts your roll unless skipping a double reaches the same threshold. In that case, choose **Continue** or press Enter to roll or **Details** to inspect the alternatives. Training starts the roll directly and retains automatic analysis for checker plays and explicit cube actions. In Tutor mode, clicking the dice or the cube can trigger a warning when the decision reaches your threshold. Previewing an alternative leaves the saved choice intact. A Competition confirmation shows only the pending choice. Reload restores pending feedback, and stale requests or engine failures preserve the checkpoint.

The **Play mode** column in Match database shows the mode and use of learning aids. You can hide or resize it independently of existing column profiles. Opening the original of an ongoing Competition match resumes Playing, including at startup. The original match and its positions become available for analysis when the match ends, or after a completed money game. Switching to Competition clears cached analysis for that source. Explicit independent library copies remain available for review.

**Learning review** distinguishes first attempts, played choices, retries, and assistance, including in mixed-mode matches. First-attempt PR uses `500 × original normalized equity loss / native-relevant decisions` at the positions actually reached. It excludes forced checker moves and applies GNUbg's strict close-cube rule to no-double choices. The review identifies its method and profile separately from GNUbg's aggregate rating of the played match. It needs GNUbg's game statistics, which exist for point matches and money games analyzed with GNUbg; a review analyzed with NeonGammon shows no first-attempt PR. Mode and assistance metadata are retained for originals, copies, and bookmarked occurrences. The match overview omits PR for matches that used learning aids.
